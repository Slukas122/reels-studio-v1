# Understand the source, then choose the format

The edit starts from the complete source, not from the first attractive quote. For spoken material, run a full-length Whisper transcription with timecoded words and preserve it beside the untouched source. Check names, numbers, negations and ambiguous phrases against audio. Sample every scene and watch each candidate with surrounding context. When speech and image disagree, investigate before writing a card. For silent footage, describe the visible sequence and use the brief or verified source facts; do not infer a quote.

## Message map

Write a short working note before cutting:

| Item | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| Central idea | What does this particular source actually say or show in one sentence? |
| Supporting ideas | Which two or three subpoints make it intelligible? Mark their source time ranges. |
| Evidence | What exact quote, action, image or checked external fact supports each point? |
| Context | Who, where, when and why must a new viewer know? Distinguish sourced facts from inference. |
| Causal order | What must come before the conclusion to keep the speaker's meaning? |
| Removable material | Which repetition or aside can go without changing the claim or tone? |
| Uncertainty | Which claims, names or visual identifications still need checking? |
| Viewer takeaway | What should a viewer be able to say after watching? |

Keep a timecoded trace from the finished line or graphic to its source. An editorial card may paraphrase a documented point, but quotation marks and subtitles are reserved for words actually spoken. If the source cannot support the desired message, change the message or obtain another source.

## Format choice

Choose the structure that lets the main idea and necessary subpoints land with the fewest unsupported editorial steps.

| Format | Use when | Structure | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spoken excerpt | A speaker has a coherent, self-contained thought | Actual line or concise context card → development → payoff | A sensational hook that the speaker never answers |
| Editorial Q&A | The source contains separable, real answers to clear questions | Short **editorial** question card → answer cut → next question only if a distinct subpoint follows | Invented interview audio, question wording that presupposes an unsupported claim, answer fragments that change meaning |
| Demonstration | An action or product is the proof | Show the result or problem → steps or contrast → outcome | Graphics covering the evidence |
| Reportage or archival | Place, event and chronology matter | Identifying context → observed action or authentic voices → consequence | False causal order or anonymous footage presented as a specific event |
| Hybrid | Speech carries the claim and graphics can clarify it | Authentic speech → a relevant diagram, label, screenshot or data beat → back to source | Graphics that repeat every spoken word or fill every pause |
| Motion-only explainer or ad | There is no useful footage, or the brief is a compact proposition supported by supplied facts | One proposition → visual progression/proof → resolution or relevant CTA | Fabricated benefits, fake interface, mandatory voiceover or forced testimonial |

Q&A cards are an editorial device, not a claim that the question was asked on camera. In a footage-led Reel, prefer a short question over the continuing picture; use a full-screen graphic only when a real chapter break helps. Use typography or a label to make the editorial framing clear when needed. Place the answer immediately after the question. If the answer needs missing context, include it briefly before the question or choose a continuous excerpt. Keep speakers and chronology identifiable. A silent motion-only Reel can use music and effects, but its visible copy must tell the complete story without audio.

## Hook and graphics checks

Before locking the hook, write `opening promise → exact payoff time/source`. Reject it if the payoff is absent, weaker than claimed, depends on a cut-away qualifier or describes a different topic. A question hook must receive an answer. A title may supply verified context; it cannot fabricate stakes. Do not require a fixed number of hook candidates.

For each proposed motion element write `viewer confusion → graphic answer → source`. Use motion when it clarifies a number, comparison, sequence, location, term, offer or transition. Prefer clean footage and a restrained caption when gesture, emotion or real action already tells the story. Cut, hold and movement rhythms follow the material; there is no mandatory visual interruption interval.
