# Minimal composition, energetic rhythm

Visual benchmark: the user-provided `motion-studio-test.mp4` (12-second, vertical kinetic showreel). Transfer its **design principles**, not its wording, colors, brand or exact timing. It uses high-contrast typography, one strong accent, clean full-frame compositions, sharp changes of scale and color, distinct motion techniques, and cuts or reveals that land with the music. There are no permanent news-style panels, decorative dashboards or dense simultaneous labels. It remains readable at phone size.

Minimalism here means **few elements at one time**, not a static edit. A frame can be bold and fast while showing only one word, one action or one useful graphic. Give each beat one visual job. Prefer a strong cut, crop, visual match or short typographic reveal before adding another layer.

## Choose the amount of motion from the source

| Format | Default treatment | When more motion helps |
|---|---|---|
| Talking head or interview | Select strong speech, direct cuts, close framing, clean timed captions and restrained music | A brief word emphasis, question or graphic that resolves confusion, identifies context or marks a real shift |
| Demonstration or reportage | Let the actual action carry the beat; cut to evidence and maintain chronology | A small arrow, term, location, comparison or verified figure precisely when needed |
| Hybrid Reel | Cut the footage story first; insert or overlay only the motion beat that explains something the footage cannot show clearly | A short diagram, real screenshot, sequence or before/after comparison backed by the source |
| Motion-only intro, explainer or ad | Full use of kinetic typography, shapes, match cuts and rhythm; one proposition or service per beat | Vary techniques to reveal relationships, never to fill empty time or imitate the reference mechanically |

The decision test for each graphic is: **What becomes clearer or more engaging because this graphic appears at this exact time?** If the answer is only “it looks professional,” remove it. If a section feels slow, check the source selection, phrase boundary, shot order, crop and sound before adding motion. A pause can be valuable when the statement needs room.

## Editing and sound

- Keep the hook truthful and visibly connected to the payoff. A good first source line often needs no separate headline.
- Cut on changes in thought, action, gaze, energy or musical phrase. Build a brisk rough cut, then restore any breath needed for comprehension. There is no mandatory cut interval.
- When music drives a motion-only piece, place the major type entrances, match cuts and logo reveal on audible accents. For speech, keep the voice dominant and let the music support, not dictate, every cut.
- Use one clear accent color, one display family and one supporting family at most. Let contrast and timing create energy instead of glows, boxes, tickers or extra labels.
- If a graphic contains text, measure its rendered bounds and test it against the target platform's interface. A bold word is useful only if it stays fully visible.

## Review question

Watch the final Reel once without sound: can the viewer identify the point and follow the visual hierarchy? Watch it again with sound: do cuts, captions, music and effects feel like one edit? Remove any graphic that competes with the speaker or proof.
